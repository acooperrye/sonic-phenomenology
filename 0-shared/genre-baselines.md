## HOW TO READ THIS

Each genre gets a baseline score on all 10 dimensions. These are NOT scores of individual songs â€” they're the CENTER OF THE GENRE. What you'd expect before you heard anything specific. The "water" that wouldn't be notable.

**Scores:** -1 to +1, where the poles match the dimension index.
**Variance:** How wide the genre spreads around that center. TIGHT = narrow range, most songs in the genre cluster here. WIDE = the genre permits a lot of variation on this dimension.
**Notable:** Where the genre's water is someone else's signal. What would be MARKED if it appeared in a different genre.

Ordered from tightest fingerprint (most distinctive, cheapest to detect) to loosest (widest variance, hardest to distinguish from other genres).

### 20. POP

The mess. Pop isn't a sound â€” it's a distribution strategy. The genre absorbs everything.

**Pop has exactly three constraints:**
1. STABILITY: tonal clarity. You can sing the hook.
2. ROUGHNESS: polished production. The surface is clean.
3. CONTINUITY: the hook returns. Cyclical architecture.

Everything else is WIDE. Pop absorbs any genre's sound as long as those three conditions hold. This is why pop is the hardest to fingerprint â€” the fingerprint is three constraints and infinite freedom everywhere else.

**What's MARKED in pop:** The three constraints are the tell. If something VIOLATES them â€” ambiguous tonality, raw production, through-composed non-return â€” it's either "art pop" (deliberate violation) or "not pop" (wrong genre classification).

### For the dictionary:

Each new song entry should note:

- Which dimensions fall within genre baseline (= water, suppress)
- Which dimensions deviate from genre baseline (= signal, amplify)
- Whether the deviation is toward another genre's water (genre-blending) or toward uncharted space (novel)

### What's missing:

**Subgenre resolution.** Jazz is too wide. Hip-hop is too wide. Pop is barely a genre. Each Tier 2 and Tier 3 genre needs subgenre fingerprints:
- Jazz: bebop, cool, free, fusion, modal, big band
- Hip-hop: boom-bap, trap, conscious, lo-fi, drill
- Pop: dance-pop, art pop, indie pop, synth-pop (already have), power pop
- EDM: house, techno, trance, dubstep, drum & bass, ambient electronic
- Metal: thrash, death, black, doom, prog, nu-metal
- Country: traditional, outlaw, bro-country, alt-country

**Era adjustment.** A "polished" recording in 1965 is "raw" by 2025 standards. The ROUGHNESS baseline needs era-correction. Similarly, loudness-war-era mastering (1995-2015) shifts the entire CONSTRAINT baseline for everything released in that window.

**Cross-genre mapping.** Some dimensional overlaps ARE the genre boundary: shoegaze and ambient share CONTINUITY and ROUGHNESS baselines. Punk and metal share ENERGY and ROUGHNESS. The overlaps define the genre transitions â€” the space between genres where art happens.
